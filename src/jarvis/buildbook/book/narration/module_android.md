- [page:pg_module_android] [show:title] This chapter is about JARVIS on your Android phone.
- [show:plain] The app is the console itself, drawn full screen by Chrome, without the address bar.
- Around it sit a few phone parts: a Talk to JARVIS tile, shortcuts, alerts sorted into Urgent, Alerts and Nudges, and JARVIS in the share sheet.
- It reads your steps, heart rate and sleep from Health Connect, so the brief can say how you slept.
- And it carries an emergency SOS: hold a button, and a fixed message with your location goes to your emergency contacts on Telegram.
- A passkey lock keeps the app closed when you've been away.
- [show:how] A phone is linked once, and afterwards it reaches JARVIS only through its door: a handful of signed routes.
- Those routes never read a conversation, the vault or mail, never approve anything, and never call a tool.
- [count:{{fact:settings.app_lock_minutes}}] The app lock asks for your passkey after {{fact:settings.app_lock_minutes}} minutes away, and only in the installed app.
- The share sheet hands a photo, a PDF or some text to the console's share tray, where you choose what happens to it.
- [show:overview] Here is the journey that matters most on a phone: an SOS.
- [node:hold] It starts with you, holding the SOS button.
- [edge:hold>countdown] Holding it starts the countdown.
- [node:countdown] Nothing is sent until the countdown ends: your hold and the countdown are the confirmation.
- [edge:countdown>cancel] If you leave the screen for good during the countdown, the path turns aside.
- [node:cancel] The SOS is cancelled, and nothing is sent at all.
- [edge:countdown>door] From the app, the send goes through the phone's door.
- [node:door] The signed door checks the request, as it checks everything the phone sends.
- [edge:door>limits] From the door, the send reaches the same guard as one from the console.
- [edge:countdown>limits] From the console, the send goes straight there.
- [node:limits] [count:{{fact:settings.sos_per_hour}}] A real SOS has its own budget, {{fact:settings.sos_per_hour}} an hour, kept apart from tests, so a test never blocks it.
- SOS is never a tool: only your held button and its countdown, or the phone's signed door, can start a send.
- [edge:limits>message] With the budget checked, the message is written.
- [node:message] Every word of it is written by code.
- [edge:message>telegram] The message travels to your contacts.
- [node:telegram] [count:{{fact:settings.sos_max_contacts}}] It goes on Telegram to up to {{fact:settings.sos_max_contacts}} joined contacts, each one once.
- [edge:message>sms] Alongside, the SMS app and the dialler are always offered.
- [node:sms] Your SMS app opens filled in for you to press Send, and the dialler is ready.
- [edge:telegram>notice] Then word comes back to you.
- [node:notice] You are told on every channel how the send went.
- [edge:notice>audit] And it is written down.
- [node:audit] The send is audited, with no location in the record.
- [show:example] In the made-up example, Anand links his phone on a Saturday: the laptop shows a short code, and he types it into the app.
- On Monday, his brief tells him how long he slept, and that the figures came from his watch.
- On Thursday evening, his scooter skids in the rain.
- [count:{{fact:settings.sos_countdown_s}}] He taps the SOS tile, and after a {{fact:settings.sos_countdown_s}}-second countdown, his contacts get a Telegram message with a map pin.
- His SMS app opens, filled in, and the console tells him how many it reached.
- [show:costs] None of this costs anything in JARVIS: the app is built by free checks for a public repository, and SOS goes through Telegram's free bot service.
- An SOS text message costs your mobile plan's usual charge, and only if you press Send.
- [show:fails] Every failed check at the phone's door gets a bare refusal, logged by the check's name only, so a flood never fills the audit trail.
- If the SOS limits can't be read, a real SOS still goes, and a refused or failed send still opens the SMS screen and the emergency number.
- An app-lock check that fails stays locked.
- [page:pg_card_c62] [show:title] The first part is the phone app itself, and the phone's door.
- [show:plain] Its phone-only parts are the tile, alerts sorted by urgency, the health sync, the SOS screen, and a DigiLocker folder you pick, whose new files it sends in.
- You link the phone once with a short code, and after that it uses only a narrow door of signed requests.
- [show:how] The link code is given only after a passkey check bound to that one offer, or a fresh Google sign-in when you have no passkey.
- [count:{{fact:src/jarvis/phone/store.py:CODE_TTL_S}}] JARVIS keeps only a keyed hash of the code, which lasts {{fact:src/jarvis/phone/store.py:CODE_TTL_S}} seconds and works once.
- [count:{{fact:src/jarvis/phone/store.py:MAX_TRIES}}] After {{fact:src/jarvis/phone/store.py:MAX_TRIES}} wrong tries, the code is void.
- Before the code is even tried, JARVIS checks the app's package name, and that its public key is a valid point on the right curve.
- [count:{{fact:settings.phone_link_max}}] The phone is then saved, with at most {{fact:settings.phone_link_max}} phones linked, and every link rings an alert on every channel.
- Each later request carries a signed header, and the door checks it in a fixed order.
- [count:{{fact:src/jarvis/phone/door.py:PER_CLIENT_MINUTE}}] First the route, no query, no cookie and the header's shape; then a flood limit of {{fact:src/jarvis/phone/door.py:PER_CLIENT_MINUTE}} a minute, and a linked phone.
- [count:{{fact:settings.phone_clock_skew_s}}] Then the clock, which must be within {{fact:settings.phone_clock_skew_s}} seconds, the body's size cap, and a one-time value never seen before.
- [count:{{fact:src/jarvis/phone/door.py:PER_PHONE_MINUTE}}] Last come the signature, and a limit of {{fact:src/jarvis/phone/door.py:PER_PHONE_MINUTE}} a minute for each phone.
- The signature covers the path, the time, that one-time value and a hash of the body, and the body must be in one exact form.
- Only then does a handler run, and an inbox file is taken only if its size and its SHA-256 hash match its first line.
- The app lock applies only to the app's own sessions, and only once a passkey was added from the app.
- Every change to the app is built and tested on GitHub with a throwaway key, while the app you install is built on your laptop.
- [show:example] In the made-up example, Meher opens Integrations on her laptop, then Phone app, touches her passkey, and gets her code.
- She types it into the app's set-up screen; the phone makes its own key inside its secure chip, and sends only the public half.
- Her laptop then shows that a phone was linked to JARVIS.
- That night, the app sends a DigiLocker PDF of her driving licence through the door, and it waits in the vault under Check details.
- When she opens the app after lunch the next day, it asks for her fingerprint before showing anything.
- [show:costs] It costs nothing: the door, the link and the lock run inside JARVIS's own server, and the build check runs on GitHub's free runners.
- [show:fails] A failed door check is logged by its name but never audited, so a flood can't fill the audit.
- If Chrome can't confirm that the site and the app belong together, the address bar shows, and the rest still works.
- And you are never locked out: from your laptop, you can switch the lock off with your passkey, or unlink the phone.
- [page:pg_card_c64] [show:title] The second part is the watch, and your sleep.
- [show:plain] If you wear a fitness watch, its steps, heart rate and sleep land in Android's Health Connect.
- The JARVIS app reads them there, and sends one short summary for each day.
- Last night's sleep becomes one line in the morning brief, and how did I sleep gets an answer by voice or chat, even with the AI off.
- No watch? Typing your own bedtime and waking time works too.
- The numbers are the watch's or the phone's estimates, never a diagnosis.
- [show:how] [count:{{fact:src/jarvis/watch/models.py:MAX_DAYS}}] The summary is checked whole: at most {{fact:src/jarvis/watch/models.py:MAX_DAYS}} recent days, and steps up to {{fact:src/jarvis/watch/models.py:MAX_STEPS}}.
- [count:{{fact:src/jarvis/watch/models.py:HEART_HIGH}}] Heart rate must lie between {{fact:src/jarvis/watch/models.py:HEART_LOW}} and {{fact:src/jarvis/watch/models.py:HEART_HIGH}} beats a minute, with at most {{fact:src/jarvis/watch/models.py:MAX_SESSIONS}} sleep sessions a day.
- Anything out of range refuses the whole summary, with fixed words that never echo a value.
- Each day is kept as one sealed record, locked with the watch's own key and bound to that day.
- To pick last night, a night you typed wins.
- Otherwise it takes the longest session that ended that morning from a single source: the watch first, then the phone, then any other health app.
- So nothing is ever counted twice.
- [count:{{fact:src/jarvis/watch/service.py:WEEK_DAYS}}] The week gives your average over the last {{fact:src/jarvis/watch/service.py:WEEK_DAYS}} nights, and how much your bedtimes wandered.
- The sleep line goes only into the console's brief, never into the e-mailed copy or onto Telegram.
- [count:{{fact:src/jarvis/watch/jobs.py:DATA_STALE_DAYS}}] If no watch data has arrived for {{fact:src/jarvis/watch/jobs.py:DATA_STALE_DAYS}} days, JARVIS tells you once, quietly.
- [count:{{fact:settings.watch_keep_days}}] Each morning, days older than {{fact:settings.watch_keep_days}} days are removed.
- [show:example] In the made-up example, Rohan's watch records his night's sleep, and the steps he walked the day before.
- Early next morning, his phone app sends that day's summary through its signed door, and his brief shows how long he slept, from his watch.
- On Sunday he asks how he slept this week, and JARVIS gives him his average night, and how much his bedtime wandered.
- [show:costs] Keeping and summarising your days costs nothing: no outside service and no AI, only fixed sentences.
- [show:fails] With no token key nothing is stored at all, and a stored day that won't open is replaced only by the phone's next sync, never by a guess.
- When no night can be read, the brief simply shows no sleep line, and the logs hold counts, never a value.
- [page:pg_card_c65] [show:title] The third part is Emergency SOS, up close.
- [show:plain] SOS lets you call for help from family or friends, in one deliberate move.
- It is not an emergency service, and the only SOS tool the brain has just tells you how it is set up.
- [show:overview] Here is one SOS press, from your hold to the record it leaves.
- [node:hold] [count:{{fact:src/jarvis/sos/service.py:HOLD_S}}] In the console, you hold the button for {{fact:src/jarvis/sos/service.py:HOLD_S}} seconds.
- [edge:hold>countdown] Then the countdown begins.
- [node:countdown] [count:{{fact:src/jarvis/sos/service.py:WEB_COUNTDOWN_S}}] The console counts down {{fact:src/jarvis/sos/service.py:WEB_COUNTDOWN_S}} seconds, and the phone's tile or shortcut counts down {{fact:settings.sos_countdown_s}}.
- [edge:countdown>send] When it ends, the send goes out.
- [node:send] Only the console's send route and the phone's signed door reach the SOS service, so no tool and no brain can start a send.
- A repeated press from the same hold gets the first answer, and sends nothing again.
- [edge:send>limits] First, the send must take a slot.
- [node:limits] [count:{{fact:settings.sos_per_day}}] A real SOS may go {{fact:settings.sos_per_hour}} times an hour and {{fact:settings.sos_per_day}} a day, and tests have their own separate budget.
- [edge:limits>sms] If the slot is refused, the path turns aside, but you are never left with nothing.
- [node:sms] The SMS screen and the dialler still open, and the phone uses the numbers it keeps, whatever the answer.
- [edge:limits>message] With a slot free, the message is written.
- [node:message] Every word comes from your own label, the time the phone fixed your place, and a rounded accuracy.
- A test says it is a test, and I'm safe carries no place at all.
- [edge:message>telegram] The fixed message travels to your contacts.
- [node:telegram] Each joined contact gets the message once, then a map pin, and each result is honest: sent, sent without the pin, failed, or not joined.
- Contacts join by pressing Start on a one-time Telegram link, and leave by sending stop.
- [edge:telegram>after] After the send, JARVIS keeps a record.
- [node:after] [count:{{fact:src/jarvis/sos/after.py:AFTER_S}}] One log entry and one audit line, with counts only, and a notice to you on every channel, waiting at most {{fact:src/jarvis/sos/after.py:AFTER_S}} seconds.
- So a slow log never holds up the word sent.
- [edge:telegram>sms] And beside Telegram, the SMS screen is offered too.
- [show:example] In the made-up example, Sunita is walking home one night when her ankle gives way on a dark lane.
- She holds the SOS tile, and after the countdown her brother and her neighbours get her SOS, with a map link and a pin.
- Her SMS app opens with the same words ready, and a button offers to call the emergency number.
- Her brother replies on Telegram that he is coming. JARVIS never reads that reply.
- [show:costs] It costs nothing in JARVIS: the messages go through JARVIS's Telegram bot, and no AI writes any word.
- [show:fails] With no contact, SOS stays off, and if the limits can't be read, a real SOS still goes, bounded by a counter in memory.
- A press that reached nobody gives its slot back, and a broken notifier, log or audit never fails the send.
- Your SOS notice has no off switch, and while the app is locked, only a real SOS goes, and the answer names no contact.
- That is the Android chapter: the console in your pocket, a narrow signed door, your nights in one line, and help one deliberate hold away.
