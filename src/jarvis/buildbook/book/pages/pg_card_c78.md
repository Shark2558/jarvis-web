## In plain words
The JARVIS app on your phone places, answers and ends calls from your own number when the cloud sends it a signed command, and the call's sound goes to your laptop over Bluetooth through Phone Link (`src/jarvis/phone/calls.py`). Its first-run screen says what it never does: hear or record a call through the phone, read your call log or contacts, or call anyone without your tap. A call card waiting for you shows on the phone too, with Approve and Decline. From you it needs the new app build installed from your laptop, one tap on Allow calls, JARVIS's notifications left on and the app's battery use set to Unrestricted (`docs/plans/r18-calls/INTEGRATION-D.md`).

## Example (made up)
Meera approves a call to her tailor on the laptop. Her phone, locked in her bag, gets a quiet push saying "JARVIS is placing the call you approved.", asks JARVIS for the command, checks the cloud's signature and dials. On Sunday a card for a call to the same tailor waits while she is out; her phone shows it with Approve and Decline. She taps Approve and unlocks the phone with her fingerprint, and the call goes ahead. Once Android holds a dial back while the phone sleeps, and she sees "Tap to let JARVIS call Tailor" instead.

## How it works
The cloud reaches the phone only by Web Push, so a command goes in two steps: `PhoneCalls` signs it and puts it in the phone's outbox in memory, then rings a doorbell, a push with a plain sentence that never carries a number or a name (`src/jarvis/phone/calls.py`). The app then asks the door's calls route, signed and checked like every door request, and may wait up to {{fact:src/jarvis/phone/calls_wire.py:WAIT_MAX_S}} seconds for a command; it also asks on every call-state change and when it opens, so a lost push only delays a command. A command is one of dial, answer, end, move the call's sound, or a waiting card, written in one exact JSON form and signed by a key the cloud makes at each start and hands over in every door answer (`CommandSigner`). The phone acts on a command only when it verifies, names this phone, is fresh and was never acted on before, checked against the same test vectors on both sides (`tests/fixtures/r18/phone_calls_vectors.json`). A dial is queued only while your approval is still live for that call, that number and you, and lives at most {{fact:src/jarvis/phone/calls.py:DIAL_MAX_S}} seconds (`src/jarvis/phone/calls.py`). A ringing call's number reaches JARVIS only while answering missed calls is on (`src/jarvis/phone/calls.py`). When you take over to the phone, the app asks to move the call's sound there; Android usually keeps that for its own dialler, so the console tells you to tap Phone on the call screen (`docs/plans/r18-calls/INTEGRATION-D.md`). Events have strict shapes, at most {{fact:src/jarvis/phone/calls_wire.py:EVENTS_MAX}} a request, and a retry is never delivered twice (`src/jarvis/phone/calls_wire.py`). Approve or Decline on the phone is your own press, as in the Calls panel, and Approve of a number never called before opens the console's Calls panel for your passkey instead (`docs/plans/r18-calls/INTEGRATION-D.md`). The permissions are asked only after your tap: place calls, answer and end them, the phone's state, and the call log only for answering with a saved-contacts rule (`android/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml`).

## What it costs
₹0. Calls run on your own phone plan, and the doorbell is Web Push, which is free (`src/jarvis/phone/calls.py`). The outbox, the events seen and the command key live in the server's memory, so nothing new is stored for them (`src/jarvis/phone/calls.py`).

## When it fails
Without the phone part, with the mobile setting off, nothing can be dialled (`src/jarvis/phone/part.py`). A dial without a live approval is never queued, also when the check itself fails (`PhoneCalls`). A dial the phone could not place, refused, without permission, expired or with no phone, ends the call at once (`src/jarvis/phone/calls.py`). When Android holds a background dial back, the app shows "Tap to let JARVIS call" with the card's name until the approval runs out (`android/app/src/main/res/values/strings.xml`). An event too old or from the future is answered but never delivered, and with answering off an incoming number is dropped on arrival (`src/jarvis/phone/calls.py`). A command that doesn't verify is never acted on (`CommandSigner`).
