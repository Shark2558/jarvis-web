## In plain words
The wardrobe is a catalogue of every piece of clothing you own, down to each sock and shoe, with where it is kept and what state it is in: ready, in use, in the laundry, away for repair and so on. Only a piece that is ready can go into an outfit (`src/jarvis/wardrobe/states.py`). You ask "where is my black belt?" and JARVIS answers from the catalogue. Every move of a piece is written into its own history that is only ever added to, never rewritten, so the catalogue stays right even when messages arrive late or twice (`src/jarvis/wardrobe/catalog/fold.py`). It is all sealed with its own key.

## Example (made up)
Gautam adds a navy kurta set: the kurta and the pyjama become two pieces plus one set, kept in "Drawer 2". He prints a sheet of small QR labels and sticks label 7 inside one of his two identical white shirts. On Monday his phone reports the white shirt went into the basket at 21:10; on Tuesday an old message from Saturday arrives saying it was clean. JARVIS ignores the old message, because the newer, confirmed one wins. On Friday he asks "where is my navy kurta?" and hears "Drawer 2, ready".

## How it works
Each piece moves between a fixed set of states, and `target_of` and `allowed` in `src/jarvis/wardrobe/states.py` decide what each kind of event may change.
`CatalogStore` keeps the catalogue as one sealed index in `wardrobe`, at most {{fact:settings.wardrobe_index_max_kb}} KB and {{fact:settings.wardrobe_max_pieces}} pieces, and each piece's history as sealed, append-only pages.
Every change happens in one transaction: `settle` fixes each event's time from the phone's capture and send times, a retry is dropped, and `fold` replays the piece's whole history in time order (`src/jarvis/wardrobe/catalog/store.py`).
In that replay an old, unconfirmed event can't undo a newer confirmed one, and an unconfirmed event that would change availability puts the piece on hold for your review instead (`src/jarvis/wardrobe/catalog/fold.py`).
`Search` finds a piece by your words without any AI: type, colour, pattern and place words narrow the list, and the rest is matched against your labels; two equal matches are told apart by place or label (`rank`).
`src/jarvis/wardrobe/catalog/labels.py` draws numbered QR labels whose codes are a keyed hash only you can produce, and `resolve` counts a scanned code only when it is one of your own assigned labels.
`Tally` gives the counts by category, and removing a piece through `CatalogBin` keeps it in Recently deleted first.
`Backup` writes a sealed copy of the wardrobe and `Restore` checks its seal before restoring in chunks, so a cut restore can run again without duplicates.
Optional AI tagging through `Tagging` is off by default, shows its cost first and is capped at ₹{{fact:settings.wardrobe_ai_tag_month_inr}} a month.

## What it costs
Nothing for the catalogue itself: it is a small amount of data in the existing database and plain code (`src/jarvis/wardrobe/catalog/store.py`). Only the optional AI tagging costs money, and only if you switch it on: its price is shown first, it counts against your monthly limit and stops at ₹{{fact:settings.wardrobe_ai_tag_month_inr}} a month (`Tagging`).

## When it fails
Without the token key the wardrobe stays off rather than storing anything in clear, and an index that can't be read is never overwritten (`CatalogStore`). A change past the size or piece limit is refused in plain words. A photo that isn't a clean JPEG of the right size is refused (`src/jarvis/wardrobe/catalog/photos.py`). A restore whose seal or format fails writes nothing (`Restore`).
