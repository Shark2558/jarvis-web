## In plain words
JARVIS is your own personal assistant, running on a server of its own in the cloud and answering only to you. You talk to it in a web page, the console, on your phone or laptop, by typing or by voice. Behind the page a brain, an AI model, reads your message together with your recent conversation, works out what you need and asks for tools such as your calendar or e-mail. Anything that would change something waits for your approval on a card. Each release comes with the Build Book, JARVIS's own record of how it is built, sealed in your vault; this book is part of it.

## Example (made up)
On Monday at 8:10 Leela opens JARVIS on her phone and types "What's on today? Put a call with the plumber at 4." JARVIS reads her calendar and answers with the day's three meetings. Under the answer a card waits: "Add event: Call the plumber, today 16:00 to 16:30", with Approve and Decline. She taps Approve, and the event is in her calendar. That evening she opens the Book panel to see how all of this works, and starts with this chapter.

## How it works
The console is one page served by JARVIS itself, `src/jarvis/api/static/console.html`, with its scripts, fonts and libraries from JARVIS, never from another website (`src/jarvis/api/console.py`). `src/jarvis/api/static/console.js` runs the chat, the brain picker and the approval cards, and `src/jarvis/api/static/console-deck.js` lays out the dock of panels. Each message goes to `Brain`, which handles one message at a time per conversation and checks your monthly limit before any paid call (`src/jarvis/core/brain.py`). `build_window` adds the recent conversation, at most {{fact:settings.session_max_turns}} turns and about {{fact:settings.session_max_chars}} characters (`src/jarvis/core/window.py`), and `DocSessionStore` keeps each conversation until you delete it (`src/jarvis/storage/sessions.py`). The brain may ask for tools for at most `tool_max_rounds` rounds ({{fact:settings.tool_max_rounds}}), and every call goes through the tool gateway, where a change waits for your approval (`src/jarvis/core/brain.py`). When an answer is cut off it is asked for once more, and one with no words at all becomes a fixed, honest line chosen by code from what happened (`src/jarvis/core/finish.py`, `src/jarvis/core/fixed_lines.py`). `src/jarvis/core/saving.py` saves the turn, and `src/jarvis/core/channel.py` notes whether it came from the console or Telegram, so that surface's own rules apply. The running version comes from one place, `src/jarvis/__init__.py`. The Build Book is JARVIS describing itself: the generator builds it from the code at each release, and the full book's pages, written by the Workshop while it builds JARVIS, are checked and compiled in by `src/jarvis/buildbook/gen/compile_pages.py`. `src/jarvis/api/static/console-book.js` opens it behind your passkey and a fresh Google sign-in, and `src/jarvis/api/static/console-book-pages.js` shows this full book a page at a time, with a flowchart for each chapter.

## What it costs
The console and the Build Book cost nothing to use, and JARVIS calls no AI to write the Book (`src/jarvis/buildbook/gen/compile_pages.py`). Each reply uses paid AI time, counted in rupees against the monthly limit you set; with the AI switched off, nothing is spent (`src/jarvis/core/brain.py`).

## When it fails
A brain error becomes a short, friendly message, never the vendor's own words (`src/jarvis/core/brain.py`). At your monthly limit no paid call is made, and the reply says so. A message that clashes with one sent from another window is not stored, and you are asked to send it again (`src/jarvis/core/saving.py`). A panel whose data doesn't arrive shows a plain line, and the rest of the console keeps working (`src/jarvis/api/static/console.js`).
