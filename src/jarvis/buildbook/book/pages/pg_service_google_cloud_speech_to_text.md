## In plain words
Google Cloud Speech-to-Text turns a recording of your voice into written words. It is how JARVIS listens whenever your laptop speech kit doesn't hear you first: after you say "Hey Jarvis", which your own device hears without sending anything, the console records what you say next and JARVIS sends that short clip to Google, which sends back the text. What goes to Google is the audio of that turn and a short list of hint words that help it spell names right: words you added in Voice settings and the names of that turn's attached files and nouns from your messages. JARVIS stores no audio of these turns and never logs what you said. With cloud listening off, the browser's own recognition is used instead. On a phone call where Google is the chosen option, what the other person says goes to Google the same way, one short clip at a time (`src/jarvis/calls/speech/build.py:build_speech`).

## Example (made up)
At 21:05 you say: "Hey Jarvis, add oat milk to groceries." The clip is three seconds long. Google returns "add oat milk to groceries" and two other guesses. JARVIS adds the item. The month's listening counter goes from 12 minutes 40 seconds to 12 minutes 55 seconds, as Google billed the short clip as a whole step. Had your laptop speech kit been awake, it would have heard the clip instead and the counter would not have moved.

## How it works
`build_voice_service` switches cloud listening on only with `voice_cloud` on, and `google_engines` signs in as JARVIS's own Google service account, so there is no extra key (`src/jarvis/voice/service.py`). `build_speech` offers Google's listening to phone calls only under the same two conditions, and reuses the voice's own connection and price list, so calls and spoken chat share one free allowance (`src/jarvis/calls/speech/build.py`). Your own listening follows your voice profile's order: the voice session's best engine, then Google, then the browser, each once; before a session, the laptop's Whisper goes first while the kit is healthy (`src/jarvis/voice/profile.py:listening_order`). A turn the laptop hears is never sent to Google or metered; with the kit off, slow or failing, Google listens as before, and the session's corrections apply whichever engine heard you (`VoiceService`). While that allowance still covers a call, the call's language uses Google before any paid option, however cheap (`src/jarvis/calls/cards.py:offered`). `GoogleSTT` sends the clip with your language, `voice_language` ({{fact:settings.voice_language}}), automatic punctuation, and Google's short-clip model for clips up to {{fact:src/jarvis/voice/google_cloud.py:SHORT_CLIP_S}} seconds, the long one otherwise (`src/jarvis/voice/google_cloud.py`). It asks for {{fact:src/jarvis/voice/google_cloud.py:MAX_ALTERNATIVES}} guesses, which costs nothing extra; the other guesses help the "understood as" check (`_alternatives`). Hint words go in a fixed order: the base list, the honorific, this turn's hints, up to `voice_hint_words` ({{fact:settings.voice_hint_words}}), then your own words (`VoiceService`). If Google refuses a request that carried your own words, `VoiceService` tries once more with the base list alone, so one bad word never stops listening. Each call has {{fact:src/jarvis/voice/google_cloud.py:DEADLINE_S}} seconds in all, sign-in included, and is never retried for being slow (`GoogleSTT`). `VoiceService` checks the free allowance first, `voice_stt_free_seconds` ({{fact:settings.voice_stt_free_seconds}} seconds a month for the deployment); a priced call is checked against your monthly limit, then counted on the time Google says it billed. The voice session's comparison asks Google alone, with your words and without your corrections, for at most `voice_session_compare_minutes` ({{fact:settings.voice_session_compare_minutes}} minutes) of listening: inside this month's free minutes each clip is free-only and never priced, and beyond them JARVIS shows the price in rupees and sends nothing until you type YES (`src/jarvis/voice/session_compare.py`).

## What it costs
Nothing in rupees inside the free allowance: JARVIS claims only {{fact:settings.voice_stt_free_seconds}} seconds of listening a month, a margin below Google's own free amount. Above it, listening is priced at `voice_price_stt_usd_per_min` ({{fact:settings.voice_price_stt_usd_per_min}} US dollars a minute), turned into rupees and counted against your monthly limit. Google may bill a short clip in whole steps, and its allowance resets on Pacific-time months. Every turn your laptop hears costs nothing and leaves the allowance untouched (`VoiceService`). The voice session's comparison lets Google hear at most {{fact:settings.voice_session_compare_minutes}} minutes: free inside the allowance, and past it you see the price in rupees and type YES before Google hears a clip (`voice_session_compare_minutes`).

## When it fails
Google slow, busy or unreachable: the console falls back to the browser's own recognition. A service switched off in your Google project is remembered as unavailable for {{fact:src/jarvis/voice/service.py:DOWN_TTL_S}} seconds, with no calls meanwhile (`VoiceService`). The laptop speech kit off, asleep or failing: Google listens instead, with no message (`VoiceService`). A clip with no speech is reported as "no speech". At your monthly limit, a priced call is refused before it is made.
